Who this is for

You do not need a large audience. One post that reaches the right person is worth more to us than a list that scrolls past. The people who do well with Superdock are:

  • Mac app reviewers and YouTubers. The Chrome profile feature demos in about fifteen seconds and people react to it, because they have lived with the problem for years.
  • Newsletter writers covering productivity, design or development, where half the readers keep a work profile and a personal one open all day.
  • People who answer questions. Reddit, Ask Different, MacRumors and Quora are full of unanswered posts asking for exactly this. A genuine answer that happens to include your link earns for months.
  • IT and agency teams who set up Macs for other people and get asked the same Chrome question every time.

What actually converts

We track what works, so here is what we have seen rather than what sounds good:

  • Show the dock, not the settings. Three Chrome profiles, three icons, three avatars. That single image does more than a paragraph.
  • Name the alternative honestly. Separate Chrome instances solve the same problem and cost a second login and roughly three times the memory. Saying so builds trust and still lands the point.
  • Lead with the free part. The dock, previews, the switcher and per-display docks are free forever. Only the profile icons are paid. Readers who install first buy more often than readers asked to buy first.
  • Use the exact phrase people search. "Separate Dock icon for each Chrome profile on Mac" is what they type. Say it in your title.

What we ask

Be honest about what Superdock does and does not do. Say that the dock itself is free and that the licence unlocks the Chrome profile icons. Disclose that your link earns you a commission, because most places require it and every reader deserves it. Do not bid on our brand name in paid search, and do not post your link where self-promotion is against the rules.

Beyond that, write what you actually think. A review that names the limitations converts better than one that does not, and we would rather have the honest one.
